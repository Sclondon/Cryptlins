# Cryptlins

A bare bulb in a brick basement, and the little monsters that live under it. A virtual pet: each hatches from an egg,
runs about, eats, plays, sleeps, makes a mess and looks at you. Godot 4.7 (GL Compatibility), made for phones and
the web, and drawn like a PlayStation game.

It lives by the clock on the wall, whether the game is open or not: a full belly lasts most of a day, it sleeps for
about seven hours in the twenty-four, and a baby is grown in a day and a half.

It starts as an egg in the lamplight. The lamp warms it slowly, the heat lamp faster, a rubbing finger fastest, and a
tap makes it knock back. It cracks twice on the way, an eye looks out of the second crack, and then it hatches. The
eggs are all plain eggs, a little rounder or taller by their kind; what is painted on the shell says what is inside:

It hatches a **baby**: small, big-headed and squeaky. After a day and a half it grows up, to a size of its own (0.8
to 1.3 times the usual).

Each cryptlin also has two **natures** (greedy, playful, shy, sleepy, cuddly, bold, grumpy or restless) that bend
its needs and habits and decide how it takes being picked up or pushed, a **favourite food**, a food it **won't
eat**, a **favourite toy**, and its own idea of a good home: the **ground** it is happiest on, how it likes the
**lamp**, two or three **things it likes having about**, and one it **cannot stand**. Its kind leans it one way or
another; the rest is its own.

Nothing about a cryptlin is ever written on the screen. How it feels shows in its eyes, its ears, the hang of its
head, and a small drawn **face** that pops up over it when its feeling changes (happy, excited, loving, hungry,
bored, sleepy, grumpy, scared, sad, disgusted, poorly). What it shows you of its tastes is written down in the **notebook**
(a "?" until then), and marked with a heart or a cross on the thing itself.

## The room and the things in it

Everything the keeper has rides a wheel at the bottom of the screen, as the things themselves: the hub is below the
screen, so only the top of the rim shows, with the middle thing highest and nearest, its neighbours lower and turned
away, and the rest sinking off the bottom corners. No wheel is drawn: only the floating things, with the room showing
between them. The scanner is first, then the broom, food, toys, the egg, things for the room, nests and trays of ground, and the
notebook last. A sideways drag turns it, and a flick keeps it running; it stops at the first thing and at the last,
and comes to rest with a thing at the top.
Tap a thing to use it, or drag it up off the wheel and let go where you want it. What is out in the room is not on the wheel: a thing stood in the room, the nest and the ground that are in use and the toy on the floor leave it, the rest close up over the gap, and it comes back to its place when the thing is put away or another nest or ground takes its place. To put a thing or the toy away, pick it up and carry it down onto the wheel: while the finger is down there the thing is gone from the room and the wheel turns to show its place and makes room for it (`Den._offer`, `Shelf.bring`); let go there (anywhere in the lower part of the strip the wheel is in, its corners too) and it is put away, let go anywhere else and it has only been moved. A cryptlin that was sitting on it, tucked into it or wading in the pool is turned out, grumbling (`Cryptlin.turned_out`). There is always a nest and a ground, so those are changed for another and never put away. A thing that has run out leaves it too, and is back when there is more. The tools (scanner, broom, notebook) never leave it.

Left alone it wanders, sits and stares at you with its head on one side, slinks off to watch from a shelter or a
dark corner, visits the things in its room, drags its nest about, gets the zoomies, dances, has a good scratch,
digs when the floor is dirt, sand or gravel (and leaves a heap), chases its own tail, sits staring up into the lamp
or the vent, stretches and yawns when it wakes, and begs with a thought bubble when it is hungry, bored or (a water
kind) dry. A few hours after a meal it goes off to a corner and leaves a **dropping**, which stays until it is
swept up with the broom and spoils the place for it meanwhile; it pees more often, and puddles dry by themselves in an hour. A
baby goes more often and wherever it happens to be. A grown one, content and left to itself, will now and then get
over-fond of its nest the way a dog does of a cushion, and is mortified if it is caught at it. M mutes the sound.

**Toys wear out.** Every kick wears the toy on the floor a little (`Ball.wear`, `Den.wear_toy`): the stone least and
the glow orb most, more from a heavy or fierce kind (a bolter, a Cerberus, a yeti) or a playful, bold, grumpy or
restless nature, less from a slight kind, a shy, sleepy or cuddly one, or a baby (`Cryptlin.rough()`). It shows:
a third of the way gone the toy is scuffed and dull, and far gone it is patched or cracked, sagging, and has lost
some of its bounce. Worn right out it comes apart at the next kick (`Den.break_toy`): a snap, the pieces flying,
whichever of them is near by pleased with itself or startled as its nature has it, and the bits left on the floor
as a mess for the broom. Three of them with the ball out get through it in about half an hour of play, one alone
in some hours: days of ordinary visits. A broken toy does not go back on the wheel. Played by itself (and for the
ball, which is free everywhere) there is another of its kind twelve hours later (`TOY_AGAIN_HOURS`). Inside a host
page a bought toy is taken from the stock when it is first brought out (the page is told, as for food and eggs)
and is the room's own from then on, kept in the save whether it is on the floor or put away; when it breaks it is
gone for good. How worn a toy is goes with it when it is put away.

## More than one
The room holds up to four, eggs and cryptlins together. Played by itself a new game starts with one egg, and the
**egg** on the wheel of things drops in another: there is no end of them. Inside a host page (the site the game is
played in) eggs are stock, each used up when it is laid, and the room is never given one for nothing: every account

the basement. A new or emptied room there stands empty, with the wheel turned to the egg and the egg bobbing,
turning and glinting on it, until the keeper taps it or drags it up into the room. Each has its own name, needs,

back, two at one bit of food share it bite by bite, and one that will not eat a thing leaves it for the others. More
than one will chase a toy. They walk round each other as they do round the things in the room, and sleep side by
side in the one nest. The marks on the wheel of things are what is known of the one last tapped or scanned.

Each pair has a **standing** with one another, kept in the save: what each thinks of the other, a little either side
of indifferent to begin with by how their natures suit (opposites grate, a grumpy one is hard going, a nature in
common or a cuddly one helps). It goes up when they sniff at each other, play chase, go after the same toy, eat side
by side and sleep in the one nest, and down when they fight. Left to themselves one will go over and sniff at
another, trail round after one it likes (a baby after anybody), or start a game of chase that turns round when it
catches up. Two that get on sleep in a heap; two that do not lie apart, or one takes the floor.

Two that do not get on can come to blows: over food one will not back off from, over a favourite toy, over a sniff
taken as a liberty, or because a hungry or grumpy one finds another standing too close. It is a ball of dust with
the odd limb and star coming out of it and a good deal of squeaking, over in a few seconds; the loser backs off to
the far side of the room with a little less **health**, and both think less of each other. A tap on the scuffle
(or a finger held on it, which picks one of them up), food dropped on it or the lamp swung over it breaks it up
with nobody hurt. Two that are fed, at home and on good terms all but never fight; fighting alone never takes one
below a third of its health; and a grown one does not fight a baby: it cuffs it out of the way, unhurt.

**Let go** (on a cryptlin's page of the notebook) asks first, then that one waves and walks off into the dark. No
**Let go** (on a cryptlin's page of the notebook) asks first, then that one waves and walks off into the dark. No
egg comes in its place, unless the room is left with nobody in it: played by itself, an empty room is always sent an
egg for nothing (inside a host page it waits for one of the keeper's own, as above).
## Knacks

Each kind has one thing that only it does, out of its own legend: its **knack** (`scripts/knack.gd`). It is a turn
the cryptlin takes now and then, of its own accord, like wandering off or having a scratch (`State.TRICK`, with a
weight in `Cryptlin._decide`), with its own antics and noises, and for some a small real effect on the room or on
another of them that soon wears off. One that needs something that is not there (another of them at a loose end, a
toy, the pool) simply does not do it until there is. No knack hurts anybody, starts a fight or a pairing, or gets
round any of the room's rules; what is done to another is a spell on it (`State.SPELL`) that runs out by itself, and
picking one up, scanning it or anything else that takes it out of its turn puts everything back as it was. Nothing
says what a knack is: the keeper finds out by seeing it done, and from then on it is written on that cryptlin's page
of the notebook ("Its knack", a "?" until then).

## Health, and dying

Each has a **health**, 0 to 100, kept with its needs. It wears away while its belly is empty (all of it in four
days of that: `STARVE_HOURS`), a lost fight takes a little, and it comes back over a couple of days fed and rested
(`MEND_HOURS`). From a full belly with nobody visiting that is between four and five days to the end, by the same
`_wear()` whether the game is open or shut. The scanner has a sixth note for it. Under 40 it is **poorly**: it drags
itself about with its head down, and the poorly face goes up over it. Nothing says so in words.

At no health it dies. It lies down where it is and goes still; a moment later a pale ghost of it gets up, drifts up
and out through the ceiling, and a small **grave marker** with a band of its own colour stands where it lay. The
marker is a thing in the room like any other (drag it about, drag it down to the wheel to put it away, tap it there to
bring it back out; the others will go and sit by it) but it is never bought or sold: it is on the wheel only while one
is put away. Its page stays in the notebook, after those of the living and marked as gone: what it was, how long it
lived, and what was ever found out about it. One that died while the game was shut is found lying there when it is

## Pairing off

Two **grown** cryptlins that both think well of each other (70 or more, each of the other), both fed, well, rested
and at home in the room, will now and then pair off. One goes over to the other, clambers up on it and gives it the
same foolish full-body wiggle a nest gets, hearts and all; that is all there is to see, and they stop dead, mortified,
if the keeper taps one, calls them, picks one up, scans one or changes the lamp, in which case nothing comes of it.
Left to it, one of the two lays an egg in the nest six hours later by the clock (`EGG_HOURS`), if the room has space
for one more by then; in a full room nothing is laid. One asleep beside an egg broods it. Neither pairs again for
three days (`PAIR_REST_DAYS`); with the game shut, a pair that would takes eight hours or so on average to get round
to it (`PAIR_AWAY_HOURS`). So a happy pair makes an egg about every three or four days, and what each thinks of the
other and the room's four places are the brakes.

`Den.would_pair()` is the one gate, and every way into the pairing goes through it (`Cryptlin._enter` turns away
anything else): never a baby, in either place; never unless each thinks well of the other, so that one being willing
is not enough; never close kin, which is a parent with its own young or two with a parent in common (each resident
has a number, and those of its two parents).

The egg is of the kind of one parent (there are no cross-bred bodies), and what hatches has that parent's size of
head for its kind. It wears the coat of the other parent, markings and all, and has the first of its two natures
from one of them; the rest of its look and its tastes come from a seed mixed out of both of theirs. Its page in the notebook names its parents.
next opened, and goes then. Nothing dies of old age.
Phone-first: on an upright phone the room is narrow, deep and tall with the lamp near the top of the screen, the
wheel of things is bigger and shows three to five of them at a time, touch targets are generous, and nothing needs a
keyboard or hover.

Each cryptlin is shaped by its kind and a seed, both kept in the save, and no two of a kind come out alike:

- **Proportions**: the size of its head, of its eyes and how far apart they sit, a slight or a stout build, long
  or short limbs, ears and tail, and more or less of whatever its kind wears (horns, antlers, a comb, a crest, a
  hat and beard, a cap, wings, feelers, a length of neck).
- **Markings**, painted on its coat: spots, patches, stripes, a pale belly, socks, a blaze, freckles, dark points,
  one over another now and then, and some plain.
- **Odd things**, by chance and only where they suit the kind: a tuft, whiskers, a snaggle tooth, a torn ear, one
  eye bigger than the other, eyes of two colours, a horn or antler broken short.
- **Colour**: fur and eyes from its kind's own colours, each drifting a little in hue and brightness.

A seed of a million or less is one from before there were more colours, and keeps to the colours its kind had
then, so a cryptlin from an old save is still the colour it was; new eggs have seeds above that and the wider
choice. `Cryptlin.colours()` and the first draws in `_build()` are as they always were; everything since comes
from `Cryptlin.looks()`, which has a generator of its own.

## How it works

- `scripts/cryptlin.gd`: the creature. `KINDS` holds what sets the thirty kinds apart in behaviour (which
  live in water, which dig, which fly), and there is one `_build_<kind>()` for each body; they all fill the same rig (two leg pivots, two
  arms, ears, eyes, mouth, tail, and for some a pair of wings that beat by themselves or two more heads that follow
  the first), so the brain and the animation are shared. Its body is spheres, capsules and cones under a few pivots;
  all animation is procedural (gait from speed, squash-and-stretch on a spring, head and pupils tracking a gaze
  point, blinks, ear twitches). States set pose goals and `_animate()` eases toward them. The brain is a small state
  machine: urgent things (tiredness, a full bladder, food, a thrown ball, the moving light) interrupt, otherwise it
  picks something by weighted chance. `profile()` turns a kind and a seed into its natures and tastes, always in the
  same order, so a saved cryptlin stays who it was. `_wear()` is time passing, used both live and for time away.
  A kind that hops where the rest walk says so in its build function (`hops = "rabbit"`): `_hop()` keeps the time of
  the hop from its speed and `_hop_drive()` gives it its ground in the hops and none between, so that it is no faster
  or slower for it, and the pose through the hop is the manner's own (`_hop_rabbit()`; another manner is another name
  and another such function).
  A kind with `"fly"` in `KINDS` (the chance each second on the move) takes short hops on the wing (`_flit`, with `flight` for how hard it takes off and how little it weighs in the air), perches on what is in `ROOSTS`, and flutters down instead of dropping when something calls it away.
- `scripts/egg.gd`: the egg, the picture painted on its shell, its warmth, cracks and hatching.
- `scripts/main.gd` (`Den`): the room, the lamp, the touches, the scanner and the save (`user://cryptlin.json`).
  `residents` is the list of those that live there (`scripts/resident.gd`: an egg or a cryptlin, and what the save
  keeps of it), `pets()` the cryptlins among them, and `who()` the one in question when a single one is meant (the
  one last tapped or scanned, or else the eldest). `comfort_of()` is how well the room suits a cryptlin; `learned()`
  is told when one has shown one of its tastes. The save is version 3 (`residents` is a list; `toy` is the toy on the floor, with where it is, how worn and whether it was bought, `toybox` the bought toys that are put away, by item id, each with its wear, `scuffs` the wear on the put-away one of each kind there is no end of, and `broken` when another of such a kind is to be had: a save without them loads with no toy out and every toy as new); a version 2 save, of
  one cryptlin or one egg, loads as a room with that one in it. Each resident in the list holds `id`, `name`,
  `kind`, `seed`, `hatched`, `born`, `warmth`, `at`, `needs` (health among them), `known`, `regard` (what it thinks
  of each of the others, by their ids), `parents` and `parent_names`, `fur`, `nature` and `looks` (what it has from them),
  `paired` and `expecting` (when it last paired off, and the egg due as a result); `gone` holds the same for those
  that have died, with `died` and `lived`, and `markers` the grave markers that are put away.
- `scripts/shelf.gd`: the wheel of things along the bottom, its own little 3D picture with nothing behind it.
  `scripts/items.gd` lists what can be on it; inside a site that keeps a shop, only what comes free and what has been
  bought.
- `scripts/decor.gd`: the things that stand in the room (shelters and the pool among them) and the nests.
  The pool is a thing like the rest (item `pool`, and in the save by where the middle of its water is) but lies
  over a part of the floor, `Den.shore`: the back, the left or the right (`Den.shores()` is those that fit the room
  as it is). `Den.wade(p)` is the shape test, how far out into the water a place is from its edge (below zero on
  dry floor) and `Den.deep(p)` how deep it is there; `Den.on_dry(p, margin, clear)` is the nearest dry floor, and
  `Den.shore_by(p, back)` the edge straight across from a place. `Decor.fill()` builds it to the room, and
  `Den._flood()` lays it and moves everything else out. The same wandering line is drawn by
  `shaders/shore.gdshaderinc`, which cuts the floor away over the water (`shaders/floor.gdshader`) and shades
  `shaders/water.gdshader`, the sheet a little below it: the two have to be changed together. A cryptlin that does
  not live in water is stopped at the edge in `Cryptlin._move()`, and gets itself out (`State.FLOUNDER`) if it is
  ever further in.
  `scripts/mess.gd`: droppings, puddles, dug heaps and the bits of a broken toy. The broom is `Items.broom()`, and `Den._tick_broom()` works it. `scripts/track.gd`: a footprint, a patch of frost or snow, or a clump of toadstools (`Den.leave_print`), which is neither saved nor a mess. `scripts/scuffle.gd`: the ball of dust two of them fight in. `scripts/snack.gd`, `scripts/ball.gd`: food and toys.
- `scripts/knack.gd`: the one thing each kind does that no other does (see Knacks above).
- `scripts/notebook.gd`: the notebook. All its words are consts there (the chapters, and a line for each kind, nature and mood) and in `Knack.NOTES`, and are written as the ticket-master: anything added goes in the same voice. `fit()` sizes it to the screen and `_lay_out()` sets each chapter out over as many leaves as it needs, measuring the words; `said()` is a whole chapter. It is drawn at the screen's own size (not the room's 240 lines) in Special Elite, a typewriter face under the Apache License 2.0 (`fonts/`, with its licence). `scripts/icons.gd`: the symbols on its few buttons.
  `scripts/readout.gd`: the scanner's own lettering, five dots by seven, for its notes. The scanner itself is
  `Items.scanner()`, and `Den._aim_scanner()` holds it in a lower corner of the view (the one further from whoever it
  is pointed at), turned to lie along the line to them, and stretches its light (the lamp's haze again) out to them.
- The PlayStation look: the room is drawn into a SubViewport 240 pixels on its short side and blown up with hard
  pixels by `shaders/ps1_screen.gdshader`, which also cuts it to 15-bit colour with the console's dither pattern.
  Every solid thing uses `shaders/ps1.gdshader` (through `scripts/ps1.gd`), which snaps corners to whole pixels and
  maps textures without perspective; meshes are low-poly. The faces over their heads are sixteen dots across and
  drawn one dot to one dot of that picture. Because the room is drawn smaller than the screen, touches go through
  `Den._in_view()` before any camera ray, and `Den.screen_of()` goes the other way.
- The eyes are emissive and the room has almost no ambient light, so outside the beam the eyes are all that shows.
- `shaders/fur.gdshader` (through `Ps1.fur` and `Cryptlin._shaggy`): long hair, for the kinds that are mostly hair (the squatchling, and the yeti, whose coat is tinted blue in its shadows). The coat is streaked down the lie of the hair and drawn again a few times over, each layer pushed further off the skin, left to hang and with more of it cut away, so that what is left stands off the body in ragged locks; nothing in it is see-through. With `edge` set the locks are drawn only where the body turns away from the eye, so that the coat is one solid thing with a ragged outline and nothing is cut out of the middle of it (at 240 lines locks all over turn to speckle), and `shade` darkens it from top to bottom. The scanner takes the layers off with the coat and puts them back.
- `shaders/beam.gdshader`: the haze in the beam. `shaders/floor.gdshader`: the grounds, from world position.
  `shaders/brick.gdshader`: the walls. No art or audio files anywhere: `scripts/sfx.gd` synthesises every sound at
  start-up.

## Test panel

Five quick taps in the top-left corner (or the ` key, or `?dev` on the address of the web build) open a small panel
on the left: **<** and **>** step through every kind, **Look** rolls a new colour, build and nature, **Egg** swaps
the cryptlin for its egg, which **Hatch** then hatches at once, **Age** switches between baby and adult, and
**+1 day** lets a day go by. While it is open the notebook shows the cryptlin's tastes without your having to find
them out.
